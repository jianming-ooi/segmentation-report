Why Active Customers Dropped on the Sep 20 Build

Property 565043719 (“Oauth RevMedia – Live”) · anomaly dated 2026-08-15 · prepares a report for leadership
One-line answer. The ~23% drop in active customers between the Sep 13 and Sep 20 builds is a data artifact, not a real decline in user activity. A one-day login/authorize event storm on 2026-08-15 inflated every build whose 30-day window covered that date. On the Sep 20 build the date fell out of the window and the metric returned to its true baseline.

1. Metric definition

  • Active customer — a customer_id with at least one linked user_id (joined via bos_customer_users) that produced reading behavior (cdp_at_user_behavior) inside the trailing 30-day window.
  • Window — rolling 30 days, evaluated at each build cut-off (inclusive both ends).
  • Reading time — last_seen_time (event grain, resolvable to the second).

3. What actually happened on 2026-08-15

Property 565043719 is “Oauth RevMedia – Live” (oauth.revmedia.my) — a shared OAuth / sign-in service used by RevMedia products (Tonton, Wowshop, BHarian, Waktu Solat Digital …). It is not a newspaper-reading property. On Aug 15 its volume spiked roughly 10–12× for a single day and reverted immediately.

Timeline of the spike

  • Baseline day (Aug 14) — ~7,000 users, ~5,000 sign-ins.
  • Aug 15 — storm: ~69,000 users / 83,000 sign-ins, concentrated in a short burst around 21:00–21:27 UTC, peaking ≈2,200 events/minute.
  • Behavior fingerprint: ~67% of users were one-shot (single event); only ~420 returned the next day, decaying to <170/day.
  • Failing endpoint /callbacks/google jumped 211 → 6,127 events with the error “Problem with your sign in … TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined.”
  • Aug 16 onward — volume reverts to baseline.
Diagnosis: these are the fingerprints of a login / authorize storm (bulk device pairing, an SSO retry loop, or an OAuth incident) — not organic reading behavior. The combination of an instantaneous spike, a failing callback, and mass one-shot users is not normal traffic.

5. Conclusion — the mechanism in one chain

  1. The shared OAuth property 565043719 experienced a one-day login storm on 2026-08-15 (~69k users, ~83k sign-ins, mostly Tonton-origin, mostly one-shot).
  2. Those logins created reading-behavior records dated 2026-08-15.
  3. Every 30-day segment build whose window contained Aug 15 (Sep 02, Sep 06, Sep 13) inherited these customers → counts inflated by ~37,000.
  4. The Sep 20 build starts on Aug 21, so Aug 15 falls out of the window → the metric drops back to its true baseline of ~94,000.

In short: the “drop” is the artifact leaving the window — not users leaving the platform.

6. Recommendation

  • Use for trend comparison  Report the Aug-15-excluded series: 95,755 / 97,807 / 98,621 / 94,281 (94k–99k, stable).
  • Do not compare raw  The raw series (132,964 / 134,840 / 135,819 / 94,281) is not comparable across Aug 15 — the first three are inflated.
  • Data hygiene  Confirm whether Aug 15 at property 565043719 was genuine traffic or a load/reprocess/incident. If not genuine, exclude it from all reporting and consider re-processing the affected builds.
  • Guardrail  Add an automated daily volume-anomaly check per property so a single-day spike cannot silently propagate into 30 days of segments.

7. Caveats

  • Timezone. Timestamps are treated as UTC (matching the Z suffix in the data). If the intended cut-off is local (GMT+7), the day boundaries shift and the numbers will change.
  • Customer linkage. Two linkage paths differ slightly: via the view’s customer_id = 45,093 customers, via the bos_customer_users bridge = 44,762 (~0.7% gap). The bridge figure is used here for consistency with the active-customer definition.
  • Root cause not yet confirmed. Whether the Aug 15 spike was a campaign, a bulk device-pairing, or an OAuth defect has not been verified at source. Suggested next checks: infrastructure/SSO logs for 20:00–22:00 UTC on Aug 15; the share of users using new tokens; and sign_in_method (currently empty at this property).
  • Correlation, not causation. The link between the sign-in storm and the inflated builds is established by timing and mechanism; it is a strong operational inference, not a controlled proof.
